## Timestep Check
The time-step check in stage 1 copies each frame, so the check isn't needed to convert anything. Its only job is to catch dropped frames or clock jumps. Stage 2 (prepping data) will resample to 0.05 s (maybe....??) assuming even spacing, and the lab's run times are compared against t. Frame-number contiguity already catches dropped frames. Perhaps we should record the largest deviation from 1/60 s in checks. We'd pick a tolerance after seeing real steps. Rounding is about 1e-6 s and a dropped frame is about 17 ms, so anything from 1e-5 to 1e-2 separates them.

`grep -rn "timeStamps" /Volumes/haruhi/phd/2017_olson_nitz_data/2017_natneuro-master/ | grep -v "load(" | less`

**`timeStamps` are recording segments, two or three per session.** The README says it outright: "timeStamps for recording segments." Every later function takes `nTimeSegments (2 or 3)` and an `iTimeSegment`, and the calls tell you what the segments are: segment 1 is the "platform", with maps made over a 300 by 300 pixel region, and segment 2 is the "track", mapped over the whole 650 by 500 frame. "Platform" is the lab's word for the open arena. So four entries is two segments, platform then track; six is a third segment, whose meaning depends on the file's tag: the rotated track in a rotation session, the dark run, the post-infusion block. The `TwoDRateMaps` array in your six-entry file has a leading dimension of 3 for exactly this reason. What the values haven't told us yet is whether the six numbers interleave as start, end pairs or go starts then ends, and whether the clock resets between segments; your snippet decides that.

**Where they come from.** `TTPostracker(spiral_events)` takes an events array as input, and `spiral_events` is 3 by 422 in your file: a Plexon event stream, timestamps with channel and value, feeding the run tracker. So the segment boundaries and the run markers most likely come from recorded events, with the hand-clicked templates deciding which path a run belongs to. That makes them more trustworthy than hand-typed times, and the checker exists because some early recordings predate the convention; those need the tracking-gap fallback.

## Data and Process Sluething

**About the files i.e. what we learned that we didn't know before**

- Each session is a tracking file and one or more analysis files, in two MATLAB formats mixed across the same day, so the reader must handle both. Missing samples are sentinels, 0 in raw and 1 in processed, with per-light flags saying lost, filled or unfilled, the back light is unfilled about a quarter of the time in the first session we opened.
- Heading is the angle from LED2 to LED1 in video coordinates with y pointing down, exactly, so LED1 is the front light and the convention is pinned. The lab's `vel` array is speed and direction of motion per light, not x and y.
- The four conditions we care about and the ones we don't are all tags in file names, with inconsistent case. Also the arena is called the platform throughout the lab's code.
- The running criterion is 10 px/s, which the lab calls about 3 cm/s, giving a first pixel scale of roughly 3.3 px per cm before any registration.

### About the segmentation

Runs were labeled by hand. `TTPostracker` is a clicking tool: start click, end click, path number, clean or dirty, reward click, with indecision points marked separately. Every downstream marker derives from that record, `spiral_events`, and `pixelDvt` carries the resulting run label per frame. `clean` is a judgment stored as the sign of the path label, not a computed criterion. `timeStamps` are row indices into the tracking, interleaved start then end, for two or three recording segments, platform, track and a variant. Also they are created before the labeling tool, probably in `TTAutoPostracker`??, and some recordings lack them. Run markers are also rows.

### About the heading.

True north is computed per recording, not per rat, as the mean raw heading on the straight north leg of two paths, the light bar's mounting angle is a calibration we might need to subtract in in a preprocessing step after the transform work is done. The lab's angular velocity is the turning rate of the direction of motion, I do not think it is of the head, so our statistics will include both of them. And `isRunningAnalysis` writes speed into the field named `angVel` and turning rate into `vel`.

## Possible Data to hide from RNN

**Direction or heading directly:**
- head_dir.
  - The front and back light positions as a pair. The line between them is the heading, and it's how the lab computed head_dir. 
  - The network should get one point per frame, not both. Front light or midpoint? Dunno.
- Where the rat is going:
  - route, goal, path_id and kind.
  - A run's start and end positions and times. End minus start is the axis of travel.
- Task-structure labels: phase, run, epoch and uninterrupted. They tell the network when a directed run is happening.
- Context: environment, condition (rotation, muscimol/ACSF) and protocol. A rotated track changes the axis. Whether the environment id may be an input is a design question for the dataset step.
- Derived motion: velocity direction, angular velocity (named in rule 1) and acceleration direction. With position-only training the network has to build these itself. A scalar speed is a maybe? Since speed can help establish a basis for different types of motion in an open field maybe vs a hallway?
- Identity:
  - subject_id, group, session_id, date, reward_rule, exclude and notes.
  - group (hippocampal or subiculum) is the same kind of label as the cell-type labels that was noted in previous discussions.
- Quality flags: filled_*, unfilled_* and the unfilled fractions. Use them to mask frames, not as inputs.
- Absolute time: t and frame give ordering only. Absolute session time reveals the epoch, because the arena comes before the track.
- Left as the only input candidate: one position point per frame, with future position as the target. (this is a huge consideration for future work... predict pos + n*t)
